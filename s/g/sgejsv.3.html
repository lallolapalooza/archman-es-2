SGEJSV

Propósito

!>
!> SGEJSV calcula la descomposición en valores singulares (SVD) de una matriz real M por N
!> [A], donde M >= N. La SVD de [A] se escribe como
!>
!>              [A] = [U] * [SIGMA] * [V]^t,
!>
!> donde [SIGMA] es una matriz N por N (M por N) que es cero excepto por sus N
!> elementos diagonales, [U] es una matriz ortonormal M por N (o M por M), y
!> [V] es una matriz ortogonal N por N. Los elementos diagonales de [SIGMA] son
!> los valores singulares de [A]. Las columnas de [U] y [V] son los vectores singulares izquierdos y
!> derechos de [A], respectivamente. Las matrices [U] y [V]
!> se calculan y almacenan en los arreglos U y V, respectivamente. La diagonal
!> de [SIGMA] se calcula y se almacena en el arreglo SVA.
!> SGEJSV a veces puede calcular valores singulares muy pequeños y sus vectores singulares mucho
!> más precisamente que otras rutinas SVD, véase más abajo en Detalles Adicionales.
!>

Parámetros

JOBA 
!>          JOBA es CHARACTER*1
!>         Especifica el nivel de precisión:
!>       = 'C': Esta opción funciona bien (alta precisión relativa) si A = B * D,
!>              con B bien condicionada y matriz diagonal D arbitraria.
!>              La precisión de la salida calculada no puede ser perjudicada por el escalado DE COLUMNA. La
!>              precisión del resultado depende del condicionamiento de
!>              B, y el procedimiento apunta a la mejor precisión teórica.
!>              El error relativo max_{i=1:N}|d sigma_i| / sigma_i está
!>              limitado por f(M,N)*epsilon* cond(B), independiente de D.
!>              La matriz de entrada se preprocesa con la QRF con pivoteo de columna.
!>              Este preprocesamiento inicial y precondicionamiento mediante
!>              una factorización QR que revela el rango es común para todos los valores de
!>              JOBA. Las acciones adicionales se especifican de la siguiente manera:
!>       = 'E': Cálculo como con 'C' con una estimación adicional del
!>              número de condición de B. Proporciona un límite de error realista.
!>       = 'F': Si A = D1 * C * D2 con escalados diagonales mal condicionados
!>              D1, D2, y matriz C bien condicionada, esta opción da
!>              mayor precisión que la opción 'C'. Si la estructura de la
!>              matriz de entrada no se conoce, y se desea precisión relativa,
!>              entonces esta opción es aconsejable. La matriz de entrada A
!>              se preprocesa con factorización QR con pivoteo COMPLETO (fila y
!>              columna).
!>       = 'G': Cálculo como con 'F' con una estimación adicional del
!>              número de condición de B, donde A=D*B. Si A tiene filas fuertemente ponderadas,
!>              entonces usar este número de condición da un límite de error demasiado pesimista.
!>       = 'A': Los valores singulares pequeños son ruido y la matriz se trata
!>              como numéricamente de rango deficiente. El error en los valores singulares
!>              calculados está limitado por f(m,n)*epsilon*||A||.
!>              La SVD calculada A = U * S * V^t restaura A hasta
!>              f(m,n)*epsilon*||A||.
!>              Esto da al procedimiento licencia para descartar (poner a cero)
!>              todos los valores singulares por debajo de N*epsilon*||A||.
!>       = 'R': Similar a 'A'. La propiedad de revelación de rango de la factorización
!>              QR inicial se utiliza para revelar (usando el factor triangular)
!>              una brecha sigma_{r+1} < epsilon * sigma_r en cuyo caso el
!>              RANGO numérico se declara como r. La SVD se calcula con
!>              límites de error absolutos, pero con mayor precisión que con 'A'.
!>

JOBU

!>          JOBU es CHARACTER*1
!>         Especifica si se calculan las columnas de U:
!>       = 'U': Se devuelven N columnas de U en el arreglo U.
!>       = 'F': se devuelve el conjunto completo de M vectores singulares izquierdos en el arreglo U.
!>       = 'W': U puede usarse como espacio de trabajo de longitud M*N. Véase la descripción
!>              de U.
!>       = 'N': U no se calcula.
!>

JOBV

!>          JOBV es CHARACTER*1
!>         Especifica si se calcula la matriz V:
!>       = 'V': Se devuelven N columnas de V en el arreglo V; las rotaciones de Jacobi
!>              no se acumulan explícitamente.
!>       = 'J': Se devuelven N columnas de V en el arreglo V, pero se calculan
!>              como el producto de rotaciones de Jacobi. Esta opción sólo está
!>              permitida si JOBU .NE. 'N', es decir, al calcular la SVD completa.
!>       = 'W': V puede usarse como espacio de trabajo de longitud N*N. Véase la descripción
!>              de V.
!>       = 'N': V no se calcula.
!>

JOBR

!>          JOBR es CHARACTER*1
!>         Especifica el RANGO para los valores singulares. Da licencia
!>         para poner a cero valores singulares positivos pequeños si están fuera
!>         del rango especificado. Si A .NE. 0 se escala de forma que el valor singular
!>         más grande de c*A sea alrededor de SQRT(BIG), BIG=SLAMCH('O'), entonces JOBR da
!>         licencia para eliminar columnas de A cuya norma en c*A es menor que
!>         SQRT(SFMIN) (para JOBR = 'R'), o menor que SMALL=SFMIN/EPSLN,
!>         donde SFMIN=SLAMCH('S'), EPSLN=SLAMCH('E').
!>       = 'N': No eliminar columnas pequeñas de c*A. Esta opción asume que
!>              BLAS y las factorizaciones QR y los resolutores triangulares
!>              están implementados para trabajar en ese rango. Si el condicionamiento de A
!>              es mayor que BIG, usar SGESVJ.
!>       = 'R': Rango RESTRINGIDO para sigma(c*A) es [SQRT(SFMIN), SQRT(BIG)]
!>              (aproximadamente, como se describe arriba). Se recomienda esta opción.
!>                                             ===========================
!>         Para calcular los valores singulares en el rango COMPLETO [SFMIN,BIG]
!>         usar SGESVJ.
!>

JOBT

!>          JOBT es CHARACTER*1
!>         Si la matriz es cuadrada, el procedimiento puede decidir usar
!>         A transpuesta si A^t parece ser mejor con respecto a la convergencia.
!>         Si la matriz no es cuadrada, JOBT se ignora. Esto está sujeto
!>         a cambios en el futuro.
!>         La decisión se basa en dos valores de entropía sobre la órbita adjunta
!>         de A^t * A. Véanse las descripciones de WORK(6) y WORK(7).
!>       = 'T': transponer si la prueba de entropía indica una convergencia posiblemente más rápida
!>         del proceso de Jacobi si A^t se toma como entrada. Si A se
!>         reemplaza con A^t, entonces el pivoteo de fila se incluye automáticamente.
!>       = 'N': no especular.
!>         Esta opción puede usarse para calcular solo los valores singulares, o la
!>         SVD completa (U, SIGMA y V). Para un solo conjunto de vectores singulares
!>         (U o V), el llamante debe proporcionar U y V, ya que una de las
!>         matrices se usa como espacio de trabajo si la matriz A se transpone.
!>         El implementador puede eliminar fácilmente esta restricción y hacer el
!>         código más complicado. Véanse las descripciones de U y V.
!>

!> SUBROUTINE SGEJSV( JOBU, JOBV, JOBA, JOBE, JOBVT, JOBP, JOBR, JOBT, M, N, A, LDA, SVA, U, LDU, V, LDV, WORK, LWORK, IWORK, INFO )
!>
!>  SGEJSV computes the singular value decomposition (SVD) of a real M-by-N
!>  matrix A, A = U*S*V^t, where S is an M-by-N diagonal matrix of the
!>  singular values and U and V are orthogonal matrices whose columns are
!>  the left and right singular vectors respectively.
!>
!>  This subroutine differs from the standard SVD interfaces in that it
!>  allows for a limited number of Jacobi sweeps for the computation of
!>  the singular values and vectors. As a trade-off, the routine is often
!>  faster than the standard SVD routine. In the case of the full SVD,
!>  it is recommended to use the LAPACK routine SGESVD.
!>
!>  Arguments
!>  =========
!>
!>  JOBU    (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the computation of the left singular vectors.
!>          = 'U': The M-by-N matrix U is computed and returned.
!>          = 'F': The M-by-M orthogonal matrix U is computed and returned.
!>                 A ONB of the orthogonal complement of the Range(A)
!>                 is included in U.
!>          = 'N': U is not computed.
!>
!>  JOBV    (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the computation of the right singular vectors.
!>          = 'V': The N-by-N orthogonal matrix V is computed and returned.
!>          = 'J': The N-by-N orthogonal matrix V is computed and returned
!>                 in a compact form.
!>          = 'N': V is not computed.
!>
!>  JOBA    (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the preconditioner of the matrix A.
!>          = 'N': No preconditioning.
!>          = 'S': The matrix A is scaled by the maximum absolute row
!>                 and column values.
!>          = 'E': The matrix A is equilibrated by row and column scaling
!>                 so that the maximum absolute row and column entries
!>                 are approximately 1.
!>          = 'G': The matrix A is equilibrated by row and column scaling
!>                 so that the maximum absolute row and column entries
!>                 are approximately 1. The equilibration is done by
!>                 perturbing the columns and rows of A.
!>
!>  JOBE    (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the preconditioner of the matrix A.
!>          = 'N': No explicit check for convergence.
!>          = 'Y': The condition number of A is computed and used to
!>                 estimate the accuracy of the computed singular values.
!>                 If the accuracy is less than EPS * lambda_max / lambda_min,
!>                 where EPS is the machine precision, the singular values
!>                 are computed again.
!>          = 'S': The matrix A is scaled by the maximum absolute row
!>                 and column values.
!>          = 'D': The matrix A is scaled by the maximum absolute row
!>                 and column values. And the maximal number of Jacobi
!>                 sweeps is reduced.
!>
!>  JOBVT   (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the transpose of V.
!>          = 'N': The matrix V is returned.
!>          = 'T': The transpose of the matrix V is returned.
!>
!>  JOBP    (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the pivoting.
!>          = 'N': No pivoting.
!>          = 'S': Partial pivoting.
!>          = 'B': Block pivoting.
!>          = '0': Use row and column pivoting.
!>
!>  JOBR    (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies the order of the singular values.
!>          = 'N': No reordering.
!>          = 'S': The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>          = 'S': The singular values are sorted in ascending order.
!>          = 'H': The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>                 The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>          = 'L': The singular values are sorted in ascending order.
!>          = 'U': The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>                 The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>          = 'A': The singular values are sorted in ascending order.
!>          = 'B': The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>                 The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>          = 'O': The singular values are sorted in ascending order.
!>          = 'T': The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>                 The singular values are sorted in descending order.
!>          = 'P': Introduce perturbation.
!>          = 'N': Do not perturb.
!>
!>  M       (input) INTEGER
!>          The number of rows of the input matrix A. M >= 0.
!>
!>  N       (input) INTEGER
!>          The number of columns of the input matrix A. M >= N >= 0.
!>
!>  A       (input/output) REAL array, dimension (LDA,N)
!>          On entry, the M-by-N matrix A.
!>
!>  LDA     (input) INTEGER
!>          The leading dimension of the array A. LDA >= max(1,M).
!>
!>  SVA     (output) REAL array, dimension (N)
!>          On exit,
!>          - For WORK(1)/WORK(2) = ONE: The singular values of A. During the
!>            computation SVA contains Euclidean column norms of the
!>            iterated matrices in the array A.
!>          - For WORK(1) .NE. WORK(2): The singular values of A are
!>            (WORK(1)/WORK(2)) * SVA(1:N). This factored form is used if
!>            sigma_max(A) overflows or if small singular values have been
!>            saved from underflow by scaling the input matrix A.
!>          - If JOBR='R' then some of the singular values may be returned
!>            as exact zeros obtained by because they are
!>            below the numerical rank threshold or are denormalized numbers.
!>
!>  U       (output) REAL array, dimension ( LDU, N ) or ( LDU, M )
!>          If JOBU = 'U', then U contains on exit the M-by-N matrix of
!>                         the left singular vectors.
!>          If JOBU = 'F', then U contains on exit the M-by-M matrix of
!>                         the left singular vectors, including an ONB
!>                         of the orthogonal complement of the Range(A).
!>          If JOBU = 'W'  .AND. (JOBV = 'V' .AND. JOBT = 'T' .AND. M = N),
!>                         then U is used as workspace if the procedure
!>                         replaces A with A^t. In that case, [V] is computed
!>                         in U as left singular vectors of A^t and then
!>                         copied back to the V array. This 'W' option is just
!>                         a reminder to the caller that in this case U is
!>                         reserved as workspace of length N*N.
!>          If JOBU = 'N'  U is not referenced, unless JOBT='T'.
!>
!>  LDU     (input) INTEGER
!>          The leading dimension of the array U,  LDU >= 1.
!>          IF  JOBU = 'U' or 'F' or 'W',  then LDU >= M.
!>
!>  V       (output) REAL array, dimension ( LDV, N )
!>          If JOBV = 'V', 'J' then V contains on exit the N-by-N matrix of
!>                         the right singular vectors;
!>          If JOBV = 'W', AND (JOBU = 'U' AND JOBT = 'T' AND M = N),
!>                         then V is used as workspace if the procedure
!>                         replaces A with A^t. In that case, [U] is computed
!>                         in V as right singular vectors of A^t and then
!>                         copied back to the U array. This 'W' option is just
!>                         a reminder to the caller that in this case V is
!>                         reserved as workspace of length N*N.
!>          If JOBV = 'N'  V is not referenced, unless JOBT='T'.
!>
!>  LDV     (input) INTEGER
!>          The leading dimension of the array V,  LDV >= 1.
!>          If JOBV = 'V' or 'J' or 'W', then LDV >= N.
!>
!>  WORK    (workspace/output) REAL array, dimension (MAX(7,LWORK))
!>          On exit,
!>          WORK(1) = SCALE = WORK(2) / WORK(1) is the scaling factor such
!>                    that SCALE*SVA(1:N) are the computed singular values
!>                    of A. (See the description of SVA().)
!>          WORK(2) = See the description of WORK(1).
!>          WORK(3) = SCONDA is an estimate for the condition number of
!>                    column equilibrated A. (If JOBA = 'E' or 'G')
!>                    SCONDA is an estimate of SQRT(||(R^t * R)^(-1)||_1).
!>                    It is computed using SPOCON. It holds
!>                    N^(-1/4) * SCONDA <= ||R^(-1)||_2 <= N^(1/4) * SCONDA
!>                    where R is the triangular factor from the QRF of A.
!>                    However, if R is truncated and the numerical rank is
!>                    determined to be strictly smaller than N, SCONDA is
!>                    returned as -1, thus indicating that the smallest
!>                    singular values might be lost.
!>
!>          If full SVD is needed, the following two condition numbers are
!>          useful for the analysis of the algorithm. They are provided for
!>          a developer/implementer who is familiar with the details of
!>          the method.
!>
!>          WORK(4) = an estimate of the scaled condition number of the
!>                    triangular factor in the first QR factorization.
!>          WORK(5) = an estimate of the scaled condition number of the
!>                    triangular factor in the second QR factorization.
!>          The following two parameters are computed if JOBT = 'T'.
!>          They are provided for a developer/implementer who is familiar
!>          with the details of the method.
!>
!>          WORK(6) = the entropy of A^t*A :: this is the Shannon entropy
!>                    of diag(A^t*A) / Trace(A^t*A) taken as point in the
!>                    probability simplex.
!>          WORK(7) = the entropy of A*A^t.
!>
!>  LWORK   (input) INTEGER
!>          Length of WORK to confirm proper allocation of work space.
!>          LWORK depends on the job:
!>
!>          If only SIGMA is needed ( JOBU = 'N', JOBV = 'N' ) and
!>            -> .. no scaled condition estimate required (JOBE = 'N'):
!>               LWORK >= max(2*M+N,4*N+1,7). This is the minimal requirement.
!>               ->> For optimal performance (blocked code) the optimal value
!>               is LWORK >= max(2*M+N,3*N+(N+1)*NB,7). Here NB is the optimal
!>               block size for SGEQP3 and SGEQRF.
!>               In general, optimal LWORK is computed as
!>               LWORK >= max(2*M+N,N+LWORK(SGEQP3),N+LWORK(SGEQRF), 7).
!>            -> .. an estimate of the scaled condition number of A is
!>               required (JOBA='E', 'G'). In this case, LWORK is the maximum
!>               of the above and N*N+4*N, i.e. LWORK >= max(2*M+N,N*N+4*N,7).
!>               ->> For optimal performance (blocked code) the optimal value
!>               is LWORK >= max(2*M+N,3*N+(N+1)*NB, N*N+4*N, 7).
!>               In general, the optimal length LWORK is computed as
!>               LWORK >= max(2*M+N,N+LWORK(SGEQP3),N+LWORK(SGEQRF),
!>                                                     N+N*N+LWORK(SPOCON),7).
!>
!>          If SIGMA and the right singular vectors are needed (JOBV = 'V'),
!>            -> the minimal requirement is LWORK >= max(2*M+N,4*N+1,7).
!>            -> For optimal performance, LWORK >= max(2*M+N,3*N+(N+1)*NB,7),
!>               where NB is the optimal block size for SGEQP3, SGEQRF, SGELQF,
!>               SORMLQ. In general, the optimal length LWORK is computed as
!>               LWORK >= max(2*M+N,N+LWORK(SGEQP3), N+LWORK(SPOCON),
!>                       N+LWORK(SGELQF), 2*N+LWORK(SGEQRF), N+LWORK(SORMLQ)).
!>
!>          If SIGMA and the left singular vectors are needed
!>            -> the minimal requirement is LWORK >= max(2*M+N,4*N+1,7).
!>            -> For optimal performance:
!>               if JOBU = 'U' :: LWORK >= max(2*M+N,3*N+(N+1)*NB,7),
!>               if JOBU = 'F' :: LWORK >= max(2*M+N,3*N+(N+1)*NB,N+M*NB,7),
!>               where NB is the optimal block size for SGEQP3, SGEQRF, SORMQR.
!>               In general, the optimal length LWORK is computed as
!>               LWORK >= max(2*M+N,N+LWORK(SGEQP3),N+LWORK(SPOCON),
!>                        2*N+LWORK(SGEQRF), N+LWORK(SORMQR)).
!>               Here LWORK(SORMQR) equals N*NB (for JOBU = 'U') or
!>               M*NB (for JOBU = 'F').
!>
!>          If the full SVD is needed: (JOBU = 'U' or JOBU = 'F') and
!>            -> if JOBV = 'V'
!>               the minimal requirement is LWORK >= max(2*M+N,6*N+2*N*N).
!>            -> if JOBV = 'J' the minimal requirement is
!>               LWORK >= max(2*M+N, 4*N+N*N,2*N+N*N+6).
!>            -> For optimal performance, LWORK should be additionally
!>               larger than N+M*NB, where NB is the optimal block size
!>               for SORMQR.
!>
!>  IWORK   (workspace) INTEGER array, dimension (MAX(3,M+3*N)).
!>          On exit,
!>          IWORK(1) = the numerical rank determined after the initial
!>                     QR factorization with pivoting. See the descriptions
!>                     of JOBA and JOBR.
!>          IWORK(2) = the number of the computed nonzero singular values
!>          IWORK(3) = if nonzero, a warning message:
!>                     If IWORK(3) = 1 then some of the column norms of A
!>                     were denormalized floats. The requested high accuracy
!>                     is not warranted by the data.
!>
!>  INFO    (output) INTEGER
!>           < 0:  if INFO = -i, then the i-th argument had an illegal value.
!>           = 0:  successful exit;
!>           > 0:  SGEJSV  did not converge in the maximal allowed number
!>                 of sweeps. The computed values may be inaccurate.
!>

Further Details:

  SGEJSV implements a preconditioned Jacobi SVD algorithm. It uses SGEQP3,
  SGEQRF, and SGELQF as preprocessors and preconditioners. Optionally, an
  additional row pivoting can be used as a preprocessor, which in some
  cases results in much higher accuracy. An example is matrix A with the
  structure A = D1 * C * D2, where D1, D2 are arbitrarily ill-conditioned
  diagonal matrices and C is well-conditioned matrix. In that case, complete
  pivoting in the first QR factorizations provides accuracy dependent on the
  condition number of C, and independent of D1, D2. Such higher accuracy is
  not completely understood theoretically, but it works well in practice.
  Further, if A can be written as A = B*D, with well-conditioned B and some
  diagonal D, then the high accuracy is guaranteed, both theoretically and
  in software, independent of D. For more details see [1], [2].
     The computational range for the singular values can be the full range

  & LAPACK routines called by SGEJSV are implemented to work in that range.

NAME

sgejsv — Singular Value Decomposition of a general single-precision matrix

SYNOPSIS

  subroutine sgejsv(JOBU, JOBV, M, N, a, lda, d, s, lwork, iwork, liwork, info)

Function/Subroutine Documentation

!> sgejsv computes the singular value decomposition (SVD) of a real
!> M-by-N matrix A, where M >= N.
!>
!> The SVD is A = U * S * V^T, where S is an M-by-N matrix with
!> nonnegative diagonal entries by construction, and U and V are
!> orthogonal matrices.
!>
!> Arguments
!> ==========
!>
!> JOBU  (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies whether to compute the matrices U and V:
!>          = 'N': None.
!>          = 'S': Some. The first min(M,N) columns of U and V are returned.
!>          = 'A': All. The matrices U and V are returned.
!>          = 'O': Some compact. The first min(M,N) columns of U and V are
!>                 returned.
!>          = 'U': Some unitary. The first min(M,N) columns of U and V are
!>                 returned.
!>
!> JOBV  (input) CHARACTER*1
!>          Specifies whether to compute the vectors V:
!>          = 'N': None.
!>          = 'S': Some. The first min(M,N) columns of V are returned.
!>          = 'A': All. The matrix V is returned in A.
!>          = 'O': Some compact. The first min(M,N) columns of V are
!>                 returned.
!>          = 'U': Some unitary. The first min(M,N) columns of V are
!>                 returned.
!>
!> M     (input) INTEGER
!>          The number of rows of A. M >= 0.
!>
!> N     (input) INTEGER
!>          The number of columns of A. N >= 0.
!>
!> a     (input/output) REAL array, dimension (LDA, N)
!>          On entry, the M-by-N matrix A.
!>          On exit,
!>          if JOBV = 'A', A is replaced by V.
!>          if JOBV = 'N', the input array A is not used.
!>          if JOBV = 'S', 'O', or 'U', the input array A is not used.
!>
!> LDA   (input) INTEGER
!>          The leading dimension of the array A. LDA >= max(1, M).
!>
!> d     (output) REAL array, dimension (min(M,N))
!>          On exit, d contains the singular values of A, sorted in
!>          descending order.
!>
!> s     (output) REAL array, dimension (min(M,N))
!>          On exit, s contains the singular values of A, sorted in
!>          descending order.
!>
!> lwork (input) INTEGER
!>          The dimension of the array WORK. lwork >= 1.
!>
!> iwork (output) INTEGER array, dimension (LIWORK)
!>          On exit, iwork is an array.
!>
!> liwork(input) INTEGER
!>          The dimension of the array IWORK. liwork >= 1.
!>
!> info  (output) INTEGER
!>          = 0: successful exit
!>          < 0: if INFO = -i, the i-th argument had an illegal value.
!>          > 0: if INFO = i, SGEJSV did not converge after i iterations.
!>
!> Details
!> =======
!>
!> The implementation of SGEJSV is based on the SVD algorithms described in
!> [1,2]. The algorithm is a block Jacobi SVD algorithm. It computes the
!> singular values and, optionally, the left and/or right singular vectors.
!>
!> The use of the Jacobi method for SVD is generally more robust than QR
!> based methods when singular values are very close to each other or very
!> small. The only drawback of the Jacobi method is its sequential nature,
!> which makes it less suitable for parallel processing.
!>
!> The main reason for the singular values to be returned in the range of
!> normalized IEEE numbers is that the spectral condition number
!> kappa(A)=sigma_max(A)/sigma_min(A) does not overflow. This code (SGEJSV)
!> is best used in this restricted range, meaning that singular values of
!> magnitude below ||A||_2 / SLAMCH('O') are returned as zeros. See JOBR for
!> details on this.
!> Further, this implementation is somewhat slower than the one described
!> in [1,2] due to replacement of some non-LAPACK components, and because
!> the choice of some tuning parameters in the iterative part (SGESVJ) is
!> left to the implementer on a particular machine.
!>
!> The rank revealing QR factorization (in this code: SGEQP3) should be
!> implemented as in [3]. We have a new version of SGEQP3 under development
!> that is more robust than the current one in LAPACK, with a cleaner cut in
!> rank deficient cases. It will be available in the SIGMA library [4].
!> If M is much larger than N, it is obvious that the initial QRF with
!> column pivoting can be preprocessed by the QRF without pivoting. That
!> well known trick is not used in SGEJSV because in some cases heavy row
!> weighting can be treated with complete pivoting. The overhead in cases
!> M much larger than N is then only due to pivoting, but the benefits in
!> terms of accuracy have prevailed. The implementer/user can incorporate
!> this extra QRF step easily. The implementer can also improve data movement
!> (matrix transpose, matrix copy, matrix transposed copy) - this
!> implementation of SGEJSV uses only the simplest, naive data movement.
!>
